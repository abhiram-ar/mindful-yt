# Download progress and parallel downloads

The user asked for a full-width progress bar, and how parallel downloads could
be shown. Measured with yt-dlp 2026.08.19 through the proxy.

## Findings

- yt-dlp always downloads the video, then the audio (`YoutubeDL.process_info`
  loops over `requested_formats`). Nothing runs in parallel.
- YouTube's adaptive formats are plain `https` with a 10 MB `http_chunk_size`,
  so `-N` (concurrent fragments) does nothing for them.
- `--extractor-args youtube:formats=dashy` turns them into
  `http_dash_segments` with 10 MB fragments. With `-N 4`, fragments failed with
  HTTP 403. yt-dlp also skips failed middle fragments by default, which leaves
  a corrupt file. Not worth building on.
- Two yt-dlp processes, one for the video and one for the audio, then merging
  ourselves: 6.7 s, including a 0.15 s merge, against 6.9 s sequential at
  720p. Most of the time is start-up and extraction. yt-dlp's own merge is
  `ffmpeg -i <video> -i <audio> -c copy -map 0:v:0 -map 1:a:0 -movflags +faststart`,
  and running it ourselves gave an identical file.
- During a download, progress hooks only see whole-stream totals. There's no
  per-fragment or per-thread state.

## Decision

Keep yt-dlp's sequential download. Show one full-width row per stream that has
started. A finished stream's bar turns green and stays, and the next stream's
row appears below it: the video first, then the audio once the video is done.
This was the user's choice. A Total row was tried and removed at the user's
request; video and audio rows are enough.

- Width is the terminal width minus one column, so a one-column shrink doesn't
  wrap the frame.
- `--progress-delta 0.2` keeps progress lines to five a second.
- `tea.View.ProgressBar` shows progress in the terminal tab or taskbar (OSC
  9;4), where supported.
